1. WHAT IS THIS PROJECT?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RetainX AI is an End-to-End Machine Learning Operations (MLOps) platform built for subscription businesses (like telecom companies). 

When customers cancel their subscriptions, it is called "Churn." Losing customers is very expensive because acquiring a new customer costs much more than retaining an existing one.

Instead of waiting for a customer to leave, RetainX AI uses historical data (the Telco Customer Churn dataset) to predict which customers are highly likely to churn *before* they actually leave. This allows the business to proactively intervene (e.g., offer a discount or better tech support).

2. WHY IS THIS PROJECT SPECIAL? (The "Wow" Factor)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Most college ML projects simply build a model, check the "accuracy," and stop. This project is built like a true Enterprise AI product:

- COST-SENSITIVE OPTIMIZATION: It doesn't care about generic "accuracy." It mathematically calculates how much a False Positive (offering a discount to someone who wasn't going to leave) costs vs. a False Negative (losing a customer you could have saved). It automatically adjusts the "Decision Threshold" (e.g., 10% instead of the default 50%) to save the business the maximum amount of money.
- SHAP EXPLAINABILITY: Business managers don't trust "black box" AI. RetainX includes an explainer (SHAP) that breaks down the exact physical force every factor played in the prediction. (e.g., "The risk is 85% because they have a Month-to-Month contract, but it was slightly lowered because they have Tech Support").
- FULL STACK ARCHITECTURE: It has a robust Python/FastAPI backend and a gorgeous, premium React/Tailwind frontend that simulates a real internal operational tool used by retention managers.


3. THE TECH STACK

4. HOW TO RUN THE PROJECT LOCALLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To run the entire pipeline from scratch, you need 3 terminal tabs.

TAB 1: Train the Model
Run this to process the dataset, train the Random Forest, calculate the business savings, and save the model:
> PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/train_model.py

TAB 2: Start the Backend API
Run this to launch the FastAPI server on port 8000:
> python3 run.py

TAB 3: Start the Frontend UI
Run this to launch the React website on port 5173:
> cd frontend
> npm run dev


5. HOW TO MAKE A PREDICTION (Using the UI)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Once the frontend is running (http://localhost:5173), you act as the Retention Manager:

Step 1: The Customer Profile Form
You are presented with a form containing 3 types of data:
- Demographics: Gender, Senior Citizen, Partner, Dependents.
- Account Information: Tenure (how many months they've been a customer), Contract type, Payment method, Paperless billing.
- Services: Internet service, Tech support, Streaming TV, Multiple lines, etc.

Step 2: Generate Risk Assessment
Fill out the form (you can just use the defaults for testing) and click "Generate Risk Assessment". The frontend sends this data to the FastAPI backend.

Step 3: Reading the Results
The right side of the screen will instantly populate with the AI's analysis:
- The Probability Score: The actual percentage likelihood that the customer will leave (e.g., 78.4%).
- The Flag (High Risk / Low Risk): The system compares the probability against the mathematically optimized threshold (e.g., 10%). If it's above the threshold, they are flagged as HIGH RISK.
- Risk Force Breakdown (The Bar Chart): This uses SHAP to explain the "Why". 
   -> Red bars pushing right mean that specific feature (like "Contract: Month-to-month") is INCREASING their likelihood of leaving.
   -> Green bars pushing left mean that specific feature (like "Tech Support: Yes") is making them MORE likely to stay.

By looking at this chart, a manager knows exactly what to offer the customer to save them!

Here is why your approach is completely different and much more advanced:

The "Standard" Way (What everyone else does)
When most people build a classification model, they just call .predict(). Under the hood, this function has a hardcoded, rigid rule:

If Probability > 50% ➡️ Say "Yes" (Churn)
If Probability < 50% ➡️ Say "No" (Stay)
This is terrible for business. Why? Because it assumes that making a mistake on a "Yes" costs the exact same amount of money as making a mistake on a "No".

Your Way: Cost-Sensitive Optimization (The "Wow" Factor)
In the real world (and in your project), mistakes are not equal!

False Positive (You flag someone who wasn't going to leave): The company might waste ₹1,000 giving them a discount they didn't need.
False Negative (You miss someone who was going to leave): The company loses a valuable subscriber, costing them ₹5,000 in lost revenue.
Because losing a customer is 5x more expensive than giving out a discount, waiting until you are 50% sure is too late.

Your code uses .predict_proba() to get the raw percentage (e.g., 36.6%). Then, during training, your script (threshold_optimizer.py) literally looped through every possible threshold (5%, 10%, 15%, etc.) and calculated the total financial cost to the business at each level.

The math proved that if the company sets their threshold at 10% instead of the default 50%, they will catch way more churners and ultimately save ₹680,000.

So yes, the fact that you discard the default 0.5 threshold, extract the raw probability, and optimize the cutoff mathematically based on financial business rules is the exact reason why this is an Enterprise-grade project.
